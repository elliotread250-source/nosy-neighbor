# SALTMARROW: PART 02, WORLD, LORE AND FACTIONS

This part is the story bible. You need it for three practical reasons: item descriptions and notes (part 44 has the full text, but it has to be consistent with this), the traders' personalities and dialogue (part 17), and the environment art, because every building on the map tells a piece of this story through what's left in it. Nothing here needs a cutscene. All of it reaches the player through objects, text, radio and place.

---

## 2.1 THE ARCHIPELAGO

Saltmarrow is a group of islands about 140 km off the coast of a fictional North Atlantic country that the game never names directly. Signs, currency and documents use English with some Norse and Gaelic place names, so it reads as somewhere between the Shetlands, the Faroes and Newfoundland without being any of them. Don't put a flag on anything. The country's government is referred to only as "the Mainland" or "the Ministry".

The main island, Saltmarrow itself, is about 1.1 km east to west and 0.9 km north to south at the scale of our map. (In fiction it's bigger; the map is a playable slice of it, with the edges blocked by cliffs, open sea and a military exclusion fence.) It has a natural harbor on the south side, a hill in the middle with a church on top, flat low-lying ground to the east where the town grew up around the cannery, and rugged moorland and cliffs to the north and west.

Three islets surround it, all reachable by boat or, at low tide at the very start of a raid, on foot across causeways and sandbars:

Gannet Rock, to the southwest: a steep, bird-covered stack with the lighthouse and the keeper's cottages.

Kirk Holm, to the east: a flat islet with the old quarantine hospital, a graveyard and a sunken chapel.

The Teeth, to the north: a cluster of rocky skerries with the wreck of a cargo ship, the MV Orla Brae, beached across two of them, and the military listening station (call sign LISTENER) on the largest.

Part 14 gives the full map layout with coordinates. This part covers what the places mean.

## 2.2 THE TOWN OF SALTMARROW, BEFORE

Before the Surge, Saltmarrow was a working fishing town of about 3,800 people, with a summer bump from tourists who came for the birds, the cliffs and the whisky. The economy ran on three things. First, the Haldane Cannery, which processed herring and mackerel from the North Atlantic fleets. Second, the ferry, which ran twice a day from the Mainland in summer and once a day in winter. Third, and the town didn't like to talk about it, the military: the listening station on the Teeth had been there since the 1960s, monitoring submarine traffic, and its staff and contractors kept the hotel and the pubs busy.

The town had a high street with a bank (the Northern Fisheries Bank), a post office, a chemist, a dentist, a butcher, two pubs (the Drowned Man and the Kittiwake), a hardware shop, a bookies, a Chinese takeaway, a fish and chip shop and a small supermarket (Lowrie's). There was a primary school, a secondary school, a cottage hospital (St. Columba's), a police station with four officers, a fire station with one engine, a lifeboat station, a power station (diesel, with a wind turbine field on the moor), a water treatment works, a small airfield for the air ambulance, a church (St. Brendan's, Church of the Islands), a Catholic chapel, a museum, a hotel (the Haldane Arms), a holiday caravan park, and a golf course on the moor that nobody ever played on because of the wind.

All of those places are on the map, or at least visible from it. The town didn't change much from about 1985 to the Surge, so the stuff in it is mostly 1980s and 1990s: CRT televisions, cassette players, analog phones, filing cabinets, Ford Escorts and Land Rover Defenders (we use fictional versions: the Rambler Estate, the Brack 110). The military station is the exception, with newer equipment from the 2010s. The time setting of the game itself is ambiguous, but the Halcyon has 2020s-era tech that's been jury-rigged and scavenged: tablets with cracked screens, solar panels, LED strip lights, a satellite uplink.

## 2.3 THE SURGE, WHAT PEOPLE KNOW

Nine years ago, on the night of 14 November, a storm came in off the Atlantic that the Met Office forecast as "severe but unremarkable." It wasn't. The storm stalled directly over the archipelago for three days. The sea rose four meters above the highest tide on record. The sky turned green. Radio stopped working. When the storm passed, roughly a third of the population was missing. Some had drowned in the flooding. Most of the missing were never found at all.

The ones who were found weren't all dead. Or, they were dead, and then they weren't. Rescue crews from the Mainland reported people walking up out of the harbor, wet and silent, their skin crusted white with something like salt. Those people were violent and didn't stop when they were shot. The Ministry evacuated the islands within a week and declared an exclusion zone.

The storm came back. Not every week, but often, and without a seasonal pattern meteorologists could explain. Each time, the sea rose with it, the green sky came back, and more of the drowned walked out of the water. The locals started calling it the Surge. The name stuck.

After the storms, the shoreline and the flooded buildings were left coated with a strange crystalline salt. Most of it was just salt. Some of it, in small amounts, was something else: faintly luminous, warm to the touch, and very valuable to someone. Within a year of the evacuation, people on the Mainland were paying fortunes for it. Nobody officially knows who's buying or what it's for. The rumors are endless: it's an energy source, a drug, a medical miracle, a weapon, a key to understanding the Surge itself.

That's why people go back.

## 2.4 THE SURGE, WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE

This is the hidden lore. The player discovers fragments of it through notes, contract chains and the listening station, and nobody ever explains all of it in one place. You need to know it so that every fragment you write is consistent.

The listening station on the Teeth was never just listening. Since the 1990s, it had been the site of a classified project called LANTERN, which used a low-frequency acoustic array on the seabed to probe something deep in the trench off the northern coast. The thing in the trench is a biological structure of enormous size, a sort of colonial organism that lives in the deep, cold water and has been there for a very long time. LANTERN's scientists called it "the Marrow" in their notes, because the first core sample they pulled from it looked like bone marrow.

The Marrow is not malicious. It's not really aware in the human sense. But it responds to sound, and LANTERN's experiments were loud. In the weeks before the first Surge, the project ran a series of high-power acoustic pulses designed to map the structure's extent. The Marrow responded. The first Surge was the response: a pulse of energy that the organism pushed up through the water column, which changed the weather, raised the sea and saturated the coast with mineralized material from its body. That's the salt. The luminous salt, Marrow Salt, is the organism's living tissue in a dormant, crystallized state.

The Hollows are the people who drowned in the first Surge and every Surge since. The salt got into them. They're dead, but the Marrow's tissue animates them in a slow, crude way. They're drawn to sound, the same way the organism is. That's why gunfire attracts them. That's why the Surge waves bring more of them. The Marrow isn't commanding them. They're just part of its body now, reaching outward the way a tide pool anemone reaches for anything that moves.

The Surge keeps coming back because the LANTERN array is still running. When the station was evacuated, the system was left in an automated mode, and it's still sending its pulses on a schedule that nobody switched off. Every pulse provokes the Marrow. Every response is a Surge. The irregular timing that baffled the meteorologists is the array's scheduling algorithm, which was designed to avoid predictable patterns that a foreign submarine could exploit.

Someone on the Mainland knows all of this. The salt buyers are a front for the same Ministry department that ran LANTERN. They want the salt because it's the only sample of the organism they can get without going into the trench. They're happy to let salvagers die collecting it.

The endgame mystery, which you won't build in v1 but whose seeds you plant: a contract chain from the trader Mother Agnes eventually leads players to the listening station's control room, where they can find the logs and, in a future update, a choice about whether to shut the array down. Shutting it down would end the Surge, and with it the salt, and with it the salvage economy that keeps the Halcyon alive. That's the moral hook for later. Don't resolve it. Just make sure nothing in v1 contradicts it.

### 2.4.1 Lore consistency rules

The word "Marrow" in-fiction refers only to the organism and to the salt (Marrow Salt). The town and island are "Saltmarrow", one word. Nobody in the Halcyon calls the organism the Marrow; that's LANTERN jargon. They say "the deep", "whatever's down there", "the old thing", or nothing at all.

Hollows never speak. They make sounds, but no words. Some notes suggest they hum, very low, when they're idle, and that the hum is in tune with the Surge's drone. That's a nice detail for the audio team (you).

The salt is always cold when it's ordinary salt and always slightly warm when it's Marrow Salt. Item descriptions should mention this.

LANTERN documents use a dry, bureaucratic tone, with redactions shown as black bars (a rich text style in UI, see part 22). They're dated with a format like "LNT/94/0331".

The Surge is never explained by any NPC as fact. Everyone on the Halcyon has a theory. Their theories are mostly wrong in interesting ways, and they reveal character.

## 2.5 THE HALCYON

The MV Halcyon was the ferry that ran the Mainland route. On the night of the first Surge, she was at anchor in the lee of Kirk Holm, waiting out the weather. Her captain, a woman named Ruth Kerrigan, kept her engines running and her bow to the waves for three days. She survived. So did the 212 people who'd fled the town onto her when the water rose.

After the evacuation, the Halcyon was supposed to be scrapped. Kerrigan and a few dozen of the survivors refused to leave her. When the salt market opened up, the Halcyon became the obvious base for salvagers: a ship big enough to live on, anchored just outside the exclusion zone, close enough to the islands to run boats in, and far enough out that the Surge's floods don't reach her. Over nine years she's become a floating town of about 400 people: salvagers, traders, mechanics, medics, a bar, a chapel, a black market, a radio station, and a council that issues its own money (Scrip) and keeps an uneasy peace.

The Halcyon is our hideout. It's where the player's stash lives, where the traders are, and where the gunsmith's workbench is. Part 17 describes the hideout upgrade stations and part 23 describes how it looks behind the menus.

### 2.5.1 The decks of the Halcyon

The car deck is now the market and workshops. Rows of stalls built from shipping pallets and tarpaulin, trader booths, the Gunsmith's Bench (the player's own corner), the boat launch ramp at the stern.

Deck 4, the old passenger lounge, is the bar: the Spindrift. Low light, a long bar made from the old cafeteria counter, mismatched chairs, a dartboard, a jukebox that plays cassettes, a wall of photos of salvagers who didn't come back.

Deck 5 is cabins, where the player's quarters are. A small cabin with a bunk, a footlocker (the stash, in fiction) and a porthole looking at the islands.

The bridge is where the council meets and where Captain Kerrigan still lives.

The radio room, behind the bridge, is where the trader Sparks runs the Halcyon's pirate radio station and listens to everything.

The engine room, deep below, is where the trader Nils runs the black market and the fuel trade.

The forward observation deck, at the bow, is where the main menu camera lives, looking out at the islands.

## 2.6 FACTIONS

There are four traders, each representing a faction on the Halcyon, plus two AI factions on the islands. Each trader buys and sells different things, has their own contract line, and has a reputation track with the player (part 17). Their personalities need to come through in every line of their dialogue and every contract description.

### 2.6.1 Ruth Kerrigan, "the Captain": The Council

Age 64. She's the ferry's captain and the head of the Halcyon's council. Practical, tired, fiercely protective of the ship and its people, and secretly worried that the salt trade is going to get everyone killed. She talks in short sentences. She doesn't swear. She calls the player "salvager" until their reputation is high, then uses their name. She's the trader for basics: starter weapons, ammunition, medical supplies, food and water, basic armor. Her contracts are about keeping the Halcyon alive: recover medical supplies from the hospital, find spare parts for the desalination plant, check on a missing boat crew.

Her station is a counter on the car deck by the bow doors, under a hand-painted sign: COUNCIL STORES. Behind her, shelves of tinned food and a map of the islands with pins in it, one pin for every salvager who didn't come back.

Sample lines:
"Back in one piece. Good. Show me what you found."
"The hospital's medical store flooded last Surge. There'll be antibiotics in the cold room if the seal held. Bring them home."
"Don't sell everything to Nils. He's not your friend."
"I've buried a lot of people your age. Don't make me do it again."

### 2.6.2 Tomasz Wren, "the Armorer": The Ironmongers

Age 41. Polish-born, came to the islands as a contractor at the listening station and got stuck there. Ex-military, a gunsmith by trade and obsession. Loves weapons the way some people love cars. Speaks quickly and technically, gets excited about parts, has strong opinions about every manufacturer. He's the trader for guns, attachments, and gunsmith upgrades. His contracts are about weapons and parts: find a specific rare optic, recover a weapons shipment from the police station's armory, test a prototype barrel in combat, kill Hollows with a specific weapon.

His booth is the most elaborate on the car deck, a workshop behind a chain-link fence with pegboards covered in parts, a bench vise, and a test-fire pit with sandbags. A radio plays jazz.

Sample lines:
"Ah. The Mako. Good gun. Bad trigger. I can fix the trigger."
"You bring me a Harrow scope with the glass intact, I'll give you a price that will make you cry."
"No, no, no. You don't put a compensator on a nine millimeter carbine. Why would you do this. Who taught you."
"I have a barrel. Sixteen inches. Cold hammer forged. Somebody died for this barrel. Treat it with respect."

### 2.6.3 Mother Agnes Fairlie, "the Chaplain": The Vigil

Age 57. She was the minister at St. Brendan's before the Surge. She's now the Halcyon's chaplain, medic and the closest thing it has to a scientist: she's been studying the salt and the Hollows for years, in a makeshift lab in the old ferry's sick bay. Calm, kind, curious, a little unsettling. She speaks gently and asks questions. She's the trader for medical gear, salt-based items (high-tier consumables made from Marrow Salt), and the "Vigil" contract line, which is the main lore line and leads toward the truth about LANTERN.

Her station is the sick bay on deck 3, half clinic and half laboratory, with jars of salt samples on every shelf, glowing faintly in the dim light. There's a cot, a desk covered in notes, and an old church lectern she uses as a writing desk.

Sample lines:
"Sit, sit. Tell me what you saw out there. Did they hum? Did you hear them hum?"
"This salt is warm. Feel it. It shouldn't be warm."
"The ones who come out of the water were someone's children once. I don't ask you to spare them. I ask you to remember it."
"There's a station on the Teeth. I think the answer is in it. I think I'm afraid of the answer."

### 2.6.4 Nils Brekke, "the Fence": The Undertow

Age 35. Norwegian, charming, untrustworthy, funny. He runs the black market from the engine room, buying and selling anything, including things the council has banned (military weapons, explosives, Marrow Salt in bulk). He's the trader who buys almost everything at a lower price, and sells the rarest and most expensive things at the highest price. His contracts are morally grey: steal from other salvagers, recover a stash before another crew does, plant a tracker on a boat, retrieve a briefcase from the Haldane Arms' safe, kill a specific type of target. He also runs the fuel trade, so he controls the boats (boat fuel and boat upgrades come from him).

His station is in the engine room, lit by work lights and the glow of the diesel engines, with a sofa that came from somewhere it shouldn't have, a minibar, and a wall of locked lockers.

Sample lines:
"My friend! Come in, come in, don't touch the red one."
"Everything has a price. Even you. Especially you, actually."
"The Captain thinks I'm a bad influence. The Captain is correct. Now, about this briefcase."
"You want military grade? I have military grade. You want to know where it came from? You do not."

### 2.6.5 Sparks (no other name given), the radio operator

Not a trader in v1 (she might become one later), but an important voice. Sparks runs Radio Halcyon from the radio room. She's young, sardonic, speaks in a fast radio-host patter. She's the in-raid voice: she broadcasts Surge warnings, extraction calls, and occasional color commentary to everyone with a radio item (every player has one in their gear by default). She's the game's announcer, essentially, but in-fiction, and her lines replace the generic "extraction available" voice of other games.

Sample in-raid lines:
"Halcyon to all crews. Pressure's dropping fast to the southwest. Surge in five minutes. You know what that means. Move."
"Boat's waiting at the cannery slip. Ninety seconds. Don't make the nice man wait."
"Somebody's having a party at the bank. I can hear it from here. Stay safe, kids."
"Salt's lit up on the Kirk Holm shore. Big batch. Every crew on the map heard me say that, by the way."
"That's the last boat out of the harbor. Anyone still on the island after this, it's been nice knowing you."

### 2.6.6 The Wardens (AI faction, Tier 2)

After the evacuation, a group of former listening station security contractors and some locals who refused to leave formed a militia that holds the military station on the Teeth and the power station on the main island. They call themselves the Wardens. They believe they're guarding the islands for the Mainland, which may or may not be true (the Ministry has never confirmed it). They're hostile to everyone, well armed, coordinated, and wear a mix of military gear and oilskins with a white painted chevron on the shoulder. They're the elite human AI enemy. They don't respawn, there are a limited number per raid, they patrol in groups of three to five, they use cover and grenades, and they carry the best loot on the map. Part 13 has the AI design.

### 2.6.7 The Hollows (AI faction)

The drowned dead, animated by the salt. Part 13 has the full taxonomy: Shamblers, Wailers, Crawlers, Bloats, Brutes, and the two bosses (the Ferryman and the Lantern Choir).

## 2.7 THE TOWN'S STORY IN ITS PLACES

Every location on the map tells a mini-story through environmental detail. When you build these (part 14), plant these stories through prop placement, notes and decals. Here's the full set. Each one needs at least one note item in the world (written out in part 44) and two or three environmental details.

The Northern Fisheries Bank, high street. The manager stayed behind to lock the vault on the night of the Surge. He's still there: a Hollow in a suit, in the vault. The vault is a loot room with a timed lock (part 14). Environmental story: the manager's desk has a letter to his wife he never finished, the safe deposit boxes have been half-looted by an earlier crew who died in the lobby (two skeletons in salvager gear, salt-crusted), and the vault door has scratch marks on the inside.

St. Columba's Cottage Hospital, east side. The hospital was the last place the evacuation reached. Patients in beds, IV stands, a children's ward with drawings on the walls. The cold room in the basement still has medicine if the seal held, which depends on whether the basement is flooded this raid. Environmental story: a nurse's station logbook with the last shift's notes, getting more frightened entry by entry, a barricaded door upstairs where staff held out, and one bed in the children's ward that's been made up neatly, with flowers in a vase that someone has been replacing. (Who? Mother Agnes. The players can find out through her contracts.)

The Haldane Cannery, harbor east. Huge, industrial, three floors of processing lines, cold stores and offices. It's the main hot zone for Harbourside. Environmental story: the processing line is still loaded with rotting fish frozen in salt, the manager's office has union dispute papers, and the deep cold store is where the Hollows nest when the water rises.

The Drowned Man pub, harbor front. The locals' pub, low and smoky, ships in bottles behind the bar, a stuffed seabird, a darts league board with names on it. The Surge flooded it to the ceiling. Environmental story: a wedding reception was happening on the night of the first Surge. The banner, the cake, the table settings are still there, salt-crusted.

The Kittiwake pub, high street. The tourists' pub, slightly nicer. Has a beer garden with a view of the harbor and a function room upstairs. Environmental story: quiz night sheets, with the last question unanswered: "What is the deepest point of the North Atlantic?"

The police station, high street. Four officers' worth of desks, two cells, an evidence locker and a small armory. Environmental story: one of the cells has a Hollow in it, still locked in, and the custody log suggests it was a prisoner on the night of the Surge. The armory was raided early on by salvagers, but the evidence locker might still have something.

The lifeboat station, harbor west. A slipway, a boathouse with the lifeboat still in it (damaged, not usable, but it's a landmark), crew room and lockers. Environmental story: the crew's last callout log, the lifeboat left the harbor on the night of the Surge to answer a mayday from the MV Orla Brae and came back without three of its crew.

St. Brendan's church, the hill. The highest point on the island, so it never floods. It's a refuge during the Surge and a sniper's paradise. Environmental story: the church was where people went when the water rose. There are sleeping bags, candles, a hand-written list of names on a board by the door (people who came in, with ticks next to the ones who were found), and the bell tower has been barricaded from the inside by someone who rang the bell for three days. Mother Agnes was the minister here. Her vestry office has her old notes.

The Haldane Arms hotel, high street west. Three floors, a lobby, a bar, a restaurant, a ballroom, 30 rooms. The hotel was where the listening station's contractors stayed. Environmental story: room 214 was rented long-term by a LANTERN scientist, Dr. Imogen Strand, and her notes are the first real clue about the project. The room safe holds a contract item for Nils.

The listening station, LISTENER, the Teeth. A fenced compound with barracks, an operations building with a radome on top, a generator shed, and the LANTERN control room deep underground, behind a blast door. Environmental story: this is where the truth is. Wardens hold it. The control room is a Tier 2 or Tier 3 feature with a multi-raid contract to open the blast door.

The MV Orla Brae wreck, the Teeth. A small cargo ship, broken across two skerries, half flooded at high tide. She was carrying something for the listening station when the Surge hit. Environmental story: the captain's log, the cargo manifest listing crates for "LNT/ACOUSTIC/SPARES", and the cargo hold full of Hollows when the water's up.

Gannet Rock lighthouse. The keeper, Old Cormac, refused to evacuate. He's not a Hollow. He's gone. His cottage has a diary that covers the first two years after the Surge, which is the best first-hand account of the storm cycle anyone has. The lamp still works, powered by a solar panel he rigged. Sometimes you see it turn on during the Surge.

Kirk Holm quarantine hospital. A Victorian fever hospital, ruined, roofless in places, with a graveyard. The chapel is sunken at one end, built on ground that's subsided. Environmental story: very old graves and very new ones, a mass grave with a recent cairn on it, and the chapel crypt floods at the first Surge wave, revealing a passage into the island's rock that leads somewhere (seed for a future update).

The power station, moor north. A diesel plant with three generator halls, a fuel tank farm and a control room. The Wardens hold it. It still runs, intermittently, which is why some lights flicker in the town. Environmental story: the Wardens' graffiti, their duty roster, a whiteboard with patrol routes (which is a useful intel item: picking it up shows Warden patrol routes on your map for that raid).

The primary school, east. Small, two classrooms, a hall, a playground. Children's drawings of the storm. Environmental story: the class's weather diary project, written by eight-year-olds, records the green sky three days before the first Surge. "Today the sky was a funny color. Miss said it was the northern lights but it was not."

The caravan park, north coast. Rows of static caravans, a shower block, a site shop. Low-tier loot spread widely, good for new players. Environmental story: one caravan is barricaded and has clearly been lived in recently. Someone's hiding on the island. (Seed: Old Cormac?)

The ferry terminal, harbor south. A waiting room, ticket office, a vehicle marshalling yard full of abandoned cars that people tried to get onto the last ferry, and the ramp. Environmental story: the abandoned queue of cars, some with suitcases still strapped on top, and a hand-written sign on the ticket office: LAST BOAT GONE. GO TO THE CHURCH.

## 2.8 HOW LORE REACHES THE PLAYER

You will build these channels. All of them are data driven.

Notes: physical items you pick up in the raid. They take one inventory cell, weigh nothing, and can be read from the inventory at any time, in raid or in the Halcyon. Once read, they're logged in the Journal (part 23) permanently, even if you lose the note itself by dying. There are 64 notes in v1 (part 44 has the full text). Some are tied to specific places, some spawn randomly from a pool.

Item descriptions: every item has a 1 to 3 sentence description written in a voice that tells you something about the world. Not "A bandage. Heals bleeding." Instead: "Field dressing from the lifeboat station's stores. The wrapper's dated two years before the Surge. Still sterile, probably." The mechanical info lives in the stat panel underneath, not in the description.

Trader dialogue: each trader has greeting lines, contract offer lines, contract completion lines, idle lines and reputation milestone lines. Part 17 sets out the system, part 44 has the text.

Radio: Sparks's broadcasts in the raid, plus occasional intercepted transmissions on special radio frequencies (an item, the Scanner, lets you tune into Warden chatter, which also reveals their positions).

Environment: everything described in section 2.7.

The Journal: a menu screen (part 23) that collects every note you've read, every contract you've done, a bestiary of every enemy type you've killed, a codex of every weapon you've used, and the lore entries unlocked by contract chains. It's the player's encyclopedia, and it's permanent.

## 2.9 NAMING CONVENTIONS FOR FICTIONAL BRANDS

We need a lot of fictional manufacturers for guns, parts, and everyday items. Here they are. Use them consistently.

### 2.9.1 Weapon manufacturers

Halvorsen Arms: Norwegian, makes precision rifles and bolt actions. Clean, Scandinavian design, walnut and blued steel. (Fathom bolt action, Skua DMR.)

Wren & Daughters: a fictional British gunmaker (no relation to Tomasz, which he finds very funny). Traditional shotguns and lever actions. Beautiful wood and engraving. (Selkie over-under, Tern lever action.)

Coastline Defense Systems (CDS): an American military contractor. Modern AR-pattern rifles and carbines, polymer and aluminum, flat dark earth. (Mako AR, Gannet SMG, Skerry carbine.)

Volkov Mechanical Works (VMW): an Eastern European manufacturer of rugged, simple, rough-finished rifles and machine guns. Long-stroke piston, stamped steel, laminated wood. (Bora AK-pattern, Grom LMG, Sever pistol.)

Teller-Kaas: German, roller-delayed blowback rifles and SMGs, very precise and expensive. (Fulmar SMG, Tellerkaas G-type battle rifle called the Albatross.)

Northern Arsenal: the Mainland's own national arsenal, makes service rifles and pistols. Bullpups and boxy designs. (Kestrel 9 pistol, Petrel bullpup.)

Marlowe Firearms: an American commercial manufacturer, revolvers and pump shotguns. (Rook revolver, Breaker pump shotgun.)

Salvage-made: guns built on the Halcyon from scrap. Ugly, unreliable, cheap, charming. (The Pipe, a single shot shotgun; the Jury, a bolt-action made from a fence post and a pipe, and so on.)

### 2.9.2 Attachment manufacturers

Harrow Optical: high-end magnified optics.
Lumen Precision: red dots and holographics.
Fen Tactical: grips, handguards, rails.
Brack Industries: stocks and buffer tubes.
Quietus: suppressors.
Kittredge: muzzle brakes and compensators.
Tidewater Outfitters: civilian sporting parts, cheap and heavy.
Nordlys: weapon lights and lasers.
Ironbark: wood furniture.

### 2.9.3 Everyday brands (for props and junk items)

Lowrie's (the supermarket), Haldane (the cannery and its tinned fish, "Haldane's Finest Kippers"), Kittiwake Lager, Selkie Gin, Brine & Co. (household cleaners), Gale Force (batteries), Northlight (torches and lightbulbs), Fairweather (radios), Puffin Crisps, Tide Cola, Saltmarrow Distillery (single malt whisky, the island's famous product, very valuable as loot).

## 2.10 THE TIMELINE

Use this for dating documents and notes.

1962: The listening station is built on the Teeth.

1991: Project LANTERN begins. The seabed array is installed in 1993.

1994 to 2013: LANTERN probes "the structure" and gradually maps it. Escalating budgets, escalating pulse power.

Year 0 (in-fiction, never given a real calendar year): 9 to 14 November, LANTERN runs Operation BELLWETHER, a series of maximum-power pulses. 14 November, 22:40: the first Surge. 17 November: the storm breaks. 18 to 24 November: evacuation. 1 December: the exclusion zone is declared.

Year 1: The Surge returns 31 times. The salt market begins in the spring. The Halcyon becomes a salvage base in the summer. The Wardens form.

Years 2 to 8: The Halcyon grows. The salt trade becomes big business. Hundreds of salvagers die.

Year 9: The present. The game begins. The Surge has been coming more often lately. Mother Agnes thinks it's getting stronger.

## 2.11 SAMPLE OF THE WORLD'S TEXT VOICE

To keep every writer (you) consistent, here's one example of each kind of text, at the quality bar. Part 44 has the rest.

Item description (Marrow Salt, small cluster): "A knuckle of pale crystal that glows like a dying coal when you cup it in your hands. It's warm. Ordinary salt is never warm. Mother Agnes pays well for it, and the men on the Mainland pay better."
