# SALTMARROW: PART 51, THE HALCYON (THE MENU SHIP)

The Halcyon is home. Players see it between every raid, so it has to be the most comforting place in the game, and the most alive. This part specifies the ship as a 3D space: its layout, the camera spots for each screen (part 23.1), the NPCs and their staging, the station visuals that change with upgrades, the lighting moods, and the ambient life that makes the ship feel lived in. In Tier 1, the Halcyon is a menu backdrop; in Tier 3, it becomes walkable (part 17.6), so build it to walkable scale from the start.

---

## 51.1 THE SHIP

The MV Halcyon is a 1980s roll-on/roll-off passenger and vehicle ferry, 110 m long, 19 m wide, with a white hull gone rust-streaked, a navy blue band along the waterline, a red funnel with the council's gull stamp painted over the old ferry company's logo, a bow visor (raised and welded open), and a stern ramp (lowered into the water as the boat launch).

She's anchored 2.5 km south-southeast of Saltmarrow harbor, bow to the islands, rolling gently. Around her: a dozen small boats moored to her sides (the salvagers' boats), a floating pontoon off the stern with fuel drums, a couple of rafts with vegetable gardens in buckets (salvager self-sufficiency), and nets hung to dry off the rails.

Decks (from the bottom):

Deck 1 and 2 (below the car deck): the engine room (Nils), fuel tanks, storage, the water desalinator.
Deck 3 (the car deck): the market and workshops: trader stalls, the Gunsmith's Bench, the stern boat ramp. Open from bow to stern under a steel ceiling at 5 m. Sick bay (Mother Agnes) is in the forward section of deck 3, behind a partition.
Deck 4 (passenger deck): the Spindrift bar (the old passenger lounge), the chapel (an old quiet room), the galley (kitchen), the council chamber (an old conference room).
Deck 5 (cabins): crew cabins, the player's cabin.
Deck 6 (the top deck): the bridge, the radio room (Sparks), the forward observation deck (the title screen), the funnel, lifeboats in their davits, the Lookout station's crow's nest on the mast.

Scale note: build the ship at real scale with the modular kit's interior pieces (doors 0.8 m, corridors 1.2 m, stairs steeper than houses: 40°), so the Tier 3 walkable version just works.

## 51.2 THE CAMERA SPOTS IN DETAIL

### 51.2.1 Title: the bow observation deck (Deck 6, forward)

A small open deck at the front of the ship with a rail, a mounted pair of coin-operated binoculars (rusted), and a bench. The camera sits low behind the rail, looking north across the water at the islands at dusk: the lighthouse sweeping on Gannet Rock (left), the harbor's few lights, the church on Kirkhill silhouetted, the Teeth's radome faintly visible far right, and the cloud base heavy. The player's character (after first login) leans on the rail at the right third of the frame, looking out. Weather: matches a calm menu preset most of the time; occasionally rain or fog (10% each). Gulls glide past.

On idle (60 s), the menu music builds into the Surge drone and a distant storm wall forms on the horizon to the west, with lightning; then it fades back (part 28.10).

### 51.2.2 Main: the car deck, wide

The camera stands on a catwalk at the car deck's stern end, 3 m up, looking forward down the length of the deck: the market stalls on both sides (built from pallets, tarpaulin, shipping containers cut open, scaffolding), string lights (warm bulbs) zigzagging overhead, people moving about (20 to 30 background NPCs: 51.4), the trader stations visible (the Captain's COUNCIL STORES counter at the forward end, Tomasz's fenced workshop on the left, Nils's engine room stairway with a neon "OPEN" sign that flickers, Mother Agnes's sick bay partition with a red cross painted on canvas), and the player's character in the middle of the deck in their current outfit and loadout (holding their primary weapon low or slung). Squadmates stand beside them.

### 51.2.3 Play: the stern ramp

The camera is on the ramp's side, looking out at the open stern: the ramp lowered into the water, a Gull boat idling at its foot with its engine thumping and a boatman waving, the islands visible beyond in the gap. The squad stands on the ramp (one to three characters), checking gear (idle animations: checking mags, adjusting straps, sipping from a flask). When the raid starts, the characters walk down the ramp and board, and the camera holds as the boat pulls away: that's the transition into the loading sequence (part 23.13).

### 51.2.4 Loadout and Stash: the player's cabin (Deck 5)

A small cabin, 3 × 4 m: a bunk with a wool blanket, a fold-down desk, a porthole looking at the islands, a steel locker (open, with the loadout's gear hanging inside: the locker interior shows the equipped items as 3D props, updating with the loadout), the footlocker (the stash in fiction), a few personal items (decorations from the Cabin station, part 17.4: posters, a rug, a lamp, trophies). For Loadout, the camera frames the character standing in front of the open locker. For Stash, the camera looks down at the open footlocker from above the character's shoulder (the stash UI overlays most of this view).

### 51.2.5 Gunsmith: the workbench (Deck 3, Tomasz's corner of the car deck)

The player's own bench, beside Tomasz's workshop: a heavy wooden bench with a vise, a pegboard of tools behind (more tools appear as the Gunsmith's Bench levels up), a warm task lamp on an arm, a rubber bench mat, cleaning rods, a coffee mug, and the weapon on the mat. The camera is the orbit camera described in part 08.8. Background: Tomasz at his own bench, working on something, occasionally looking over (an idle animation) and occasionally saying a line about your build (from a set of build-aware lines: "A compensator on that? Hm." when you install one).

### 51.2.6 Traders: their stations

The Captain: COUNCIL STORES, a counter made from the old ferry information desk at the car deck's forward end, shelves of tinned food and supplies behind, the map of the islands with pins (one pin per lost salvager: the pins increase as the server's total player deaths grow: a subtle shared-world detail), a clipboard, a thermos. She stands behind the counter, sometimes writing, sometimes looking at the map.

Tomasz: a workshop behind a chain-link fence on the car deck's port side: pegboards of parts, a test-fire pit with sandbags (he occasionally test-fires into it: a muffled bang that makes nearby NPCs flinch), a radio on a shelf (silent until TOM20, then jazz), and a vise with a receiver in it. He's in a canvas apron with safety glasses pushed up on his head.

Mother Agnes: the sick bay in the forward section of deck 3, behind a canvas partition: two cots (one with a patient sleeping), shelves of jars of salt samples glowing faintly, her desk with notes and the lectern she uses as a writing desk, a kettle on a camping stove, a cross on the wall. She wears a cardigan over a clerical collar, reading glasses on a chain. Often seated, writing, or examining a salt sample under a lamp.

Nils: the engine room, reached by a stairway from the car deck (the camera descends with the screen transition): the big diesels thrumming (two running), work lights, the sofa (a leather Chesterfield, scuffed), a minibar (a hotel minibar fridge), a wall of lockers (the red one), crates, a hanging chandelier he took from somewhere (lit, absurd). He's lounging on the sofa or standing at a crate counting Scrip, in a good leather jacket and a fisherman's beanie.

### 51.2.7 Halcyon (stations): the bridge

The ship's bridge on deck 6: the wheel, the instrument consoles (most dead, some patched with newer electronics), wide windows looking at the islands, the chart table in the middle with the hand-drawn ship schematic (the station markers overlay it, part 23.9), the Captain's mug, the ship's bell mount (empty until CAP15 returns the bell). The camera looks down at the chart table at a 40° angle.

### 51.2.8 Salvager: the cabin mirror

The cabin (51.2.4) with the camera behind a mirror on the wall facing the character (we see their reflection's point of view: so the camera faces the character directly, and the mirror frame borders the screen edges). Lighting is warm from the desk lamp.

### 51.2.9 Journal: the cabin desk

The cabin's fold-down desk with the notebook open, a pen, a candle or lamp, the porthole beyond. The camera looks down at the notebook; the Journal UI is the notebook's pages.

### 51.2.10 Settings: the bridge instruments

Close on the bridge's instrument panel (dials, switches): the settings UI overlays it.

## 51.3 TRANSITIONS

Moving between screens moves the camera between spots (part 22.8: 450 ms, skippable, or cut with Reduce Motion). Between distant spots (the car deck to the cabin, two decks up), use a quick cut through black with a ship's sound (a door clang, footsteps on a steel stair) rather than flying the camera through walls. Between nearby spots (the car deck to a trader's stall), a smooth camera move.

## 51.4 THE PEOPLE OF THE HALCYON

Background NPCs make the ship alive. 20 to 30 visible on the car deck at a time, spread across the ship's spots, with simple behaviors: walking between points (along splines), working at stalls (repairing a net, sorting salt, cooking at a grill, hammering), talking in pairs (gesture animations), sitting (on crates, eating), carrying things (boxes, a fuel can, a fish). Built from the same character kit as players (bodies, MetaHuman heads from the preset set, clothing), with randomized outfits, so they look like salvagers. They don't speak (ambient murmur audio from the space, part 28). They react lightly to the player character (a head turn as the camera passes).

Named NPCs with small roles (Tier 2+, for flavor and side contracts): Dora the tailor (the cosmetics stall, part 10.8), Old Hamish the chandler (sells nothing, but sits on a chair by the stern ramp and says one line when you leave: "Mind the water"), a cook (the Galley), Peter Leask (now 17, working the fuel pontoon; the boy who rang the bell, part 44 N25; he doesn't talk about it), Morag Groat (whose husband was on the Bonnie Jean, CAP04; after the contract, she's seen at the Wall of the Lost), the deck watch (two armed guards at the stern ramp).

The Wall of the Lost (in the Spindrift): a wall of photographs and notes for salvagers who didn't come back, including (after CAP10) the items you returned, and (with the Chapel station) the callsigns of your squadmates who died in raids with you.

## 51.5 STATION VISUALS

Each station upgrade changes something visible (part 23.9): Gunsmith's Bench L2 adds a second pegboard and a parts cabinet, L3 adds a precision bench light, a bore scope and a small test range lane; Stores L2 to L5 add lockers in the cabin corridor (your name on them); Locksmith L2 and L3 add a key-cutting machine and a safe; Sick Bay L2 adds a second cot and a fridge, L3 a glowing distillation rig for salt tinctures; Galley L2 adds a stove and a kettle on the car deck with a queue of NPCs; Workshop L2 and L3 add a lathe and a reloading press (with brass in bins); Boathouse levels change the boat at the stern ramp (skiff, upgraded skiff, RIB, Gull Launch); Radio Room L1 and L2 add antennas on the mast and equipment in Sparks's room; Lookout adds a crow's nest platform and a big pair of binoculars; Cabin levels decorate your cabin; Chapel adds candles to the Wall of the Lost.

Use data layers or a "station visual state" component on prop groups, keyed to the station level from the backend.

## 51.6 LIGHTING AND TIME OF DAY

The menu's time of day follows the player's local clock in four presets (part 23.1): morning (pale, misty, cool), afternoon (overcast, neutral), dusk (the default look: warm string lights against a cold blue sky, the lighthouse on), night (the ship lit warmly from inside, stars or clouds, the island dark except for a few lights). Weather variants: calm, drizzle, fog, rain (rain on the car deck's open stern, drips from the ceiling's edges).

Interior lighting: warm practical lights everywhere (string lights, desk lamps, work lights, the bar's lamps), cool daylight from portholes and the open stern, and colored accents (Nils's neon, Agnes's salt glow, the bridge's instrument lights). The Halcyon grade (part 25.4.2) is warmer than the raid.

## 51.7 AUDIO

The ship's bed: the low hum of the engines (louder near the engine room), the hull creaking with the swell, water lapping at the sides, the stern ramp's chains clinking, rain on the deck (when raining), gulls, the distant foghorn of Gannet Rock in fog. The car deck adds the market: murmured conversation (unintelligible), hammering, a grill sizzling, a radio somewhere playing old music (diegetic, faint), boots on steel. The Spindrift adds the jukebox and darts (Tier 3). Music (part 28.10) plays over the bed.

## 51.8 PERFORMANCE

The menu scene must run at 120 fps on the reference laptop at Medium (it's on screen a lot, and players will judge the game by its menus). Budget: the ship is a contained interior plus a view of the distant island (use a low-detail island proxy: an HLOD of the raid map, or a dedicated low-poly backdrop with the lighthouse and a few lights). Background NPCs use animation sharing and low LODs. Only the current camera spot's area renders at full detail: hide distant decks' content when not in view (sub-levels streamed by camera spot).

## 51.9 THE HALCYON ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

Every screen has its camera spot with correct framing at 16:9, 21:9 and 16:10 (the UI panels don't cover the subject of the shot).

Station levels visibly change the ship.

The scene holds 120 fps at Medium on the reference laptop.

The ship feels alive: at any time, at least 6 NPCs are visible doing something on the car deck, and no two adjacent NPCs play the same animation in sync.
